## Interpretation notes

### B1: the error popup itself is not the violation

A dialog can appropriately explain a failure. The intentional problem is **when** the system reveals a known stock constraint: after users have invested in choosing options. The dialog accurately reports the failed add, preserves recoverability, and never changes stock randomly. During Office Hours, confirm which specific taught concept describes this late disclosure and whether it is distinct from A2.

### C1: several features may belong to one concept

Missing labels, identical field styling, unusual order, and lack of grouping all make the form harder to interpret. They are one implemented device here, not four automatically distinct rubric concepts. Confirm the course's exact concept name and whether its distinction from A2 is defensible. The separate payment guide makes correct completion possible without changing the intentionally unlabeled fields. Revision 2 also requires manual transcription; this supports the same device rather than automatically counting as a new course concept.

### A3 and A5: friction must remain operable

A3 affects attention rather than requiring pursuit of moving click targets. A5 keeps a real, stationary cart control that is faint light gray by default and clearer on desktop hover or keyboard focus. These are deliberate visual decisions, not broken event handling. Screenshots alone cannot establish the animation speed or click operability; pair them with runtime checks.

### Concept-count review checklist

- Ask the instructor to confirm A2, B1, and C1 are sufficiently distinct, or identify the overlap.
- Confirm whether movement, visual similarity, contrast, feedback placement, and habituation are counted as distinct in this course's terminology.
- Record the instructor's exact labels and lecture references when available.
- If fewer than ten unique concepts are accepted, propose a targeted adjustment using the provided course material and obtain the user's agreement before adding behavior.
- Do not infer course coverage from an outside UX article or manufacture slide citations.
